# Design decisions

Status key: **Decided** means you said it. **Assumed** means I filled a gap and it needs
a yes or no. **Open** means it is still undecided (see `open-questions.md`).

## Current direction: upright carton (October 2026)

- **Decided.** The carton stands upright (the tipped version's 40 degree inside ramp could
  not fit the diagonal zigzag stairs). Stairs go up around the inside walls.
- **Decided.** Gallon-carton proportions (6 x 4.5 x 9.5 in), scaled to 40 ft to the top of
  the fin: 25.3 x 18.9 ft footprint, walls 30.7 ft, roof 6.9 ft, fin 2.4 ft.
- **Decided.** The spout is on a narrow end and is pulled open like a real carton (shape per
  Allison's sketch): the end of the gable hinges at the top of the wall, the roof corners and
  side flaps bend along creases, the fin lifts toward the end, and the tip ends up above the
  fin. The whole diamond on top of the spout is the opening. It is open for the look.
- **Decided.** No wrap-around slide. The slide comes out of a tear in the spout-end wall at
  the 12 ft landing and runs straight out (about 32 ft, steepest 30 degrees) into a puddle.
- **Decided.** Classic red-and-white milk carton colours (replaces blue and white).
- **Assumed.** The roof/fin split was read off a reference picture; spout tip 4.8 ft out and
  1.4 ft above the fin, crease positions scaled up from Allison's sketch.
- **Assumed.** Spiral stairs 4.5 ft clear at 31 degrees, landings at every corner plus one
  partway along each long wall (longest fall about 5.4 ft), up to a lookout floor at 24 ft.

Everything below describes the earlier tipped design.

## The piece

- **Decided.** A classic old-school gable-top milk carton, 40 ft tall, tipped over with the
  spout open. Milk flows out of the spout into a puddle on the ground. The milk looks like
  milk but is a slide.
- **Decided.** Built entirely from wood so it can burn, then painted blue and white. The
  look is a fully painted carton, not exposed scaffolding. The slide is white and milk-like
  but also wood.
- **Decided.** The carton is sunk slightly into the playa so it has a flat base.
- **Decided.** No extra structure on the side of the carton and no "lost child" panel.
- **Context.** This feeds the Black Rock City honoraria art grant letter of intent.

## Tilt

- **Decided: 50 degrees from vertical.** It was originally 30 to 45. We tried 38, 30, 45
  and 50. A bigger tilt makes the climb less steep: the surface you climb slopes
  (90 - tilt) degrees, so 50 gives 40 degrees. It also brings the spout down to about
  20 ft above grade.
- The other inner surface, the old bottom panel, slopes `tilt` degrees the other way, so
  the inside is a V-shaped trough with its low point at the base corner.

## Spout

- **Decided.** The classic built-in pull-open spout, in line with the top of the carton,
  at the end of the roof ridge, like the reference photos.
- **Decided.** The slide comes directly out of that one spout. There is no other opening.
- The roof end is pointed: the top edge runs flat to the tip and the lower edge slopes
  straight back to the body corner (your red shape). The opening is a triangle along the
  ridge, widest at the tip (your purple shape). The tip overhangs the right wall by about
  3.3 ft.

## Slide

- **Decided.** Shaped like a poured spill: it leaves the spout nearly level, rolls over a
  lip, drops steeply, then eases out flat into the puddle (your purple line).
- With the 50 degree tilt this is a drop of about 18 ft over about 32 ft of travel, with
  the steepest section near 67 degrees. That is much steeper than a playground slide
  (about 30 to 35 degrees). **Open:** see speed control in `open-questions.md`.

## Interior

- **Decided.** The interactive interior (udders, bullrings) is removed for now. Inside is a
  staircase for climbing instead.
- **Decided.** Zigzag flights: up one side, a landing, up the other side, and so on, with
  railings, so a fall can only go as far as the next landing.
- **Decided.** Wide enough for people to go up and down at the same time.
- **Decided.** Keep the zigzag flights that cross the carton's width, not straight flights
  up its length with full-width landings. (See `stair-analysis.md` for what "diagonal"
  turns out to mean.)
- **Open: stair width.** You asked about 4.5 ft and were leaning toward 5.5 ft. The
  generator says 5.5 ft does not fit as designed. See `stair-analysis.md`.
- Earlier ideas that were set aside: bullrings on rods (now wall-mounted if they come
  back), trampoline-lined floor (it bounces people sideways on a slope and is not
  burnable), climbing-net floor.

## Other assumptions

- **Entry door** in the front face (the big side face), at grade, near the low corner. A door
  in the sloped wall would not work because that wall slopes at 40 degrees.
- **Launch deck** at the top of the stairs, with a short bridge across to the spout, which
  sits in the middle of the carton's depth.
- **Slide supports** are placeholders. A 20 ft high slide needs real structure.
- **Railings** are 3.5 ft high in the drawings. The right height and baluster spacing come
  from BRC art safety and your engineer.
